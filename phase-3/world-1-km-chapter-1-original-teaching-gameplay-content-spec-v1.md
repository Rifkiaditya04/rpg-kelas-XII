# World 1 — KM Standard Chapter 1 Original Teaching / Gameplay Content Specification v1

**Status:** DRAFT — CONTENT DESIGN GATE
**Phase:** Phase 3 — Content / Gameplay Rebaseline
**Basis:** Approved Chapter 1 Competency & Coverage Matrix v1 + verified Student Book Chapter 1 scope.

## 1. Purpose

Translate the approved Chapter 1 competency map into an original Light RPG learning experience without copying textbook wording, examples, exercises, answer choices, figures, or dialogue.

This specification defines the teaching/gameplay layer before question drafting. It does not approve a final question bank or renderer changes.

## 2. Non-negotiable source/copyright boundary

The Student Book and Teacher Guide are curriculum/provenance references. Production content must be independently authored.

Allowed use:
- identify competencies and concepts;
- identify learning objectives and activity scope;
- understand the intended academic demand;
- record provenance by chapter/activity/section.

Do not reproduce:
- textbook sentences/passages;
- textbook exercises/questions;
- answer choices or answer keys copied from source;
- distinctive source examples/dialogue/figures;
- superficial rewrites that preserve a distinctive source expression.

Every game explanation, NPC dialogue, guided example, scenario, question, distractor, feedback, and practice item must be original while preserving the source-supported learning intent.

## 4. Chapter 1 narrative frame

The World 1 content should frame official communication as a practical school/work communication problem. The player encounters situations where information must be understood, evaluated, inspected, produced, answered, or transformed for another audience.

The narrative is an original game scenario inspired by the competency scope, not a recreation of textbook characters, examples, or wording.

## 5. Teaching design by competency cluster

### Cluster 1 — Receive and evaluate information (A–B)

**Teaching intent:** teach the player to identify relevant information, distinguish directly stated meaning from inferred meaning, compare messages, and justify an evaluation from available evidence.

**NPC role:** introduces a communication task and explains why careful reading/listening matters before acting.

**Guided example:** original fictional official communications with invented names, dates, purposes, and details.

**Challenge pattern:** evidence selection, explicit-vs-inferred interpretation, comparison.

**Feedback:** explain which evidence supports the interpretation; do not reveal copied textbook rationale.

### Cluster 2 — Reflect and interpret (C)

**Teaching intent:** connect information from an official communication to a realistic situation and produce a reasoned response rather than unsupported guessing.

**NPC role:** asks the player to decide what a communication means for a concrete school/work situation.

**Guided example:** original scenario with a fictional communication and a decision point.

**Challenge pattern:** scenario choice / structured response. If current renderer cannot represent the intended evidence reliably, document the gap rather than forcing an invalid MCQ.

### Cluster 3 — Inspect official communication (D–E)

**Teaching intent:** recognize structural elements, appropriate register, standard wording, and effective sentences; diagnose problems and select an appropriate repair.

**NPC role:** acts as an editor/mentor who teaches how to inspect a communication before it is sent.

**Guided example:** original fictional document containing deliberately designed issues.

**Challenge pattern:** document inspection, MCQ, multi-select, repair/diagnostic selection.

### Cluster 4 — Produce and respond to official communication (F–G)

**Teaching intent:** select appropriate standard wording, construct effective sentences, adapt a response to purpose/audience, and understand the electronic-mail communication context.

**NPC role:** provides a task and a communication goal, then guides the player through planning before production.

**Guided example:** original fictional school/work request and an email-response situation.

**Challenge pattern:** guided reconstruction/selection using existing renderer capabilities.

**Renderer gap note:** fully authored free-text composition may require a future interaction type. Do not silently add it during this gate.

### Cluster 5 — Transform information into public communication (H)

**Teaching intent:** extract essential information from a circular communication and organize it for an announcement/publication context.

**NPC role:** explains audience and information-priority decisions.

**Guided example:** original fictional circular and an original announcement/poster brief.

**Challenge pattern:** information selection, ordering/selection, document inspection.

## 6. Learn / Context design rules

Each mission must provide enough explanation to make the challenge a learning activity rather than a blind quiz.

A Learn panel should contain:
1. what the player is trying to understand;
2. why the skill matters in the scenario;
3. a short original explanation;
4. one original guided example;
5. a transition into the challenge.

Learn/context must not change XP, Mission Complete, or prior learning evidence.

## 7. Mission design rules

- Mission boundaries follow narrative and competency coverage, not an arbitrary question count.
- A mission may combine multiple activities when their competencies naturally form one learning task.
- A broad activity may receive multiple challenges.
- Challenge quantity is determined after detailed source/coverage analysis.
- Every production item requires provenance metadata.
- Current question types remain preferred unless a documented renderer gap is demonstrated.

## 8. Learning evidence

Evidence should identify the competency/topic tested and the observed response pattern. It must remain separate from game XP.

Training Room content may later target the same competency using new original examples and practice tasks.

## 9. Question authoring gate

Before any question is drafted:
- identify the exact competency anchor;
- identify the intended cognitive demand;
- define the original scenario/document;
- define the evidence needed for the answer;
- write the question independently;
- write independent distractors where applicable;
- write original feedback/explanation;
- attach provenance without copying source wording.

## 10. Renderer impact assessment

Current decision: **retain renderer and mission engine**.

Potential gap: authored-response/composition for F/G. This remains an open design/technical decision. It requires a separate proposal and approval if current interaction types cannot faithfully support the competency.

No renderer modification is authorized by this document.
