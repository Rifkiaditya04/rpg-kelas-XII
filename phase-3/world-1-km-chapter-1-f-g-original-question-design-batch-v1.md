## F — Write official communication
### F-Q01 — Audience and purpose
**Skill:** identify the first planning decision for a formal school/work-related message.
**Type:** single-select.
**Original scenario:** A student must request an internship-related document from a school office.
**Options:**
- A. Determine the recipient and the purpose of the request.
- B. Make the message as long as possible.
- C. Begin with casual language to sound friendly.
- D. Add unrelated personal details.
**Canonical:** A.
**Explanation:** The recipient and purpose establish what the message needs to communicate and how it should be framed.

### F-Q02 — Clarity of required information
**Skill:** select the formulation that makes a required date clear in a formal request.
**Type:** single-select.
**Original scenario:** The student needs a school document before a stated internship deadline.
**Options:**
- A. State the needed document and the required time clearly.
- B. Hint at the deadline without stating it.
- C. Omit the timing because the office can guess it.
- D. Replace the request with a general statement about internships.
**Canonical:** A.
**Explanation:** Important information should be explicit enough for the recipient to act without guessing.

### F-Q03 — Formal register
**Skill:** choose language appropriate to a formal school administration request.
**Type:** single-select.
**Original scenario:** A draft contains a highly casual greeting and slang.
**Options:**
- A. Revise the wording so it matches a formal administrative context.
- B. Keep the slang because the recipient is a school employee.
- C. Add more slang to make the message warmer.
- D. Remove the purpose of the request instead.
**Canonical:** A.
**Explanation:** Register should fit the relationship, purpose, and institutional context.

## G — Reply through email
### G-Q01 — Complete confirmation
**Skill:** identify the essential content of a formal event-confirmation reply.
**Type:** single-select.
**Original scenario:** A school office asks a student to confirm attendance at a career preparation event.
**Options:**
- A. Confirm attendance and identify the event being confirmed.
- B. Reply only that the message was read.
- C. Reply with an unrelated question.
- D. Send an emoji without text.
**Canonical:** A.
**Explanation:** The reply should make its purpose and the information being confirmed clear to the recipient.

### G-Q02 — Appropriate tone
**Skill:** select an email response appropriate for a school-office recipient.
**Type:** single-select.
**Original scenario:** The student accepts an invitation from a school administration office.
**Options:**
- A. Use concise, polite language suited to the institutional recipient.
- B. Use slang because email is a digital medium.
- C. Use only abbreviations and omit the greeting.
- D. Write as if responding to a close friend.
**Canonical:** A.
**Explanation:** The communication medium does not by itself determine the level of formality; recipient and purpose matter.

### G-Q03 — Information completeness
**Skill:** identify a response that contains the information the recipient needs.
**Type:** single-select.
**Original scenario:** The office needs confirmation that the student will attend a specific event on the stated date.
**Options:**
- A. Confirm attendance and refer clearly to the relevant event/date.
- B. Say only "I understand."
- C. Say only "Thanks."
- D. Leave the response blank and rely on the subject line.
**Canonical:** A.
**Explanation:** A useful confirmation contains the information needed to resolve the request without another clarification cycle.
